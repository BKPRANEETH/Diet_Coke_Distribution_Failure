# Improving Diet Coke / Low-Sugar OTIF and Building Packaging-Supply Resilience in Bangalore
### A Lean Six Sigma Green Belt (DMAIC) Project — Coca-Cola secondary distribution, Jan–May 2026

---

## 1. Executive summary

In April 2026, Diet Coke vanished from shelves and quick-commerce apps across Bengaluru and other Indian metros. The trigger was a global **aluminium-can shortage** (Strait-of-Hormuz / Gulf disruption), but the reason a packaging shock became a *distribution collapse* was structural: **Diet Coke is sold almost entirely in cans**, with no PET/glass fallback and no substitution playbook.

Using a calibrated simulation of the Bangalore low-sugar distribution network (71,195 order lines, Jan 1–May 31 2026), this project applies DMAIC to:

- **Measure** the collapse — low-sugar OTIF fell from **88.8% to 20.8%**; process capability fell from **2.72σ to 0.69σ** (≈792,000 DPMO).
- **Analyze** the cause — **86.8%** of crisis failures trace to one cause (can unavailability); **CAN OTIF fell to 3%** while **PET held at ~85%** (χ² = 17,623, p < 0.001).
- **Quantify** the impact — **≈₹1.83 crore** of contribution margin (₹5.9 cr revenue) lost in six weeks.
- **Improve** — three Green-Belt interventions recover **≈₹1.45 crore (80%)** and lift crisis OTIF to **81.1%**.
- **Control** — a p-chart and supply-risk early-warning dashboard to prevent recurrence.

A secondary but important finding: the **pre-crisis baseline (89%) was already 5.8 pp below** the 95% FMCG benchmark, indicating routine service gaps independent of the shock.

---

## 2. Business context

India's beverage demand is shifting structurally toward low/zero sugar — from ~5% of Coca-Cola India volumes (2020) to ~30% (2025), with Diet Coke roughly doubling year-on-year, against a zero-sugar segment projected at ~$4.7 bn by 2030. Coca-Cola India revenue was ~US$533 m in FY25. Bangalore is among the most Diet-Coke-intensive metros (urban millennial / Gen-Z "daily ritual" demand and dense quick-commerce). The portfolio was scaling hard precisely when the packaging shock hit.

## 3. Problem statement

> Diet Coke / low-sugar OTIF in the Bangalore secondary-distribution network is structurally exposed to aluminium-can supply shocks. Quantify the service and margin impact of the April 2026 shortage, isolate the root cause, and design Green-Belt-executable resilience interventions to restore service to target and protect margin.

**CTQ (Critical-to-Quality):** OTIF (On-Time **and** In-Full) at order-line level — an all-or-nothing metric (a late *or* short line fails).

## 4. Objectives

1. Establish a defensible OTIF / fill-rate measurement baseline and quantify the gap to the 95% benchmark.
2. Identify and statistically validate the dominant root cause of the crisis collapse.
3. Quantify lost revenue and contribution margin.
4. Design and prioritise interventions a Green Belt can execute or pilot.
5. Quantify recoverable margin and the achievable OTIF recovery.
6. Install controls and an early-warning system to prevent recurrence.

## 5. Research questions

- How far did OTIF and fill rate fall, by how much, and over what window?
- Is the collapse concentrated by pack format, brand, channel, or SKU?
- Is the pack-format effect statistically significant or noise?
- What is the rupee impact, and how much is *controllable* vs a pure physical shock?
- Which interventions give the best recovery per unit of effort (RICE)?

## 6. Methodology

DMAIC, supported by: descriptive + segmentation analysis, **Pareto**, **Fishbone + 5-Whys**, a **chi-square test of independence** (pack format × OTIF), **FMEA** for residual supply-risk ranking, **RICE** for prioritisation, **unit-economics / contribution-margin bridges** for impact, a **counterfactual recovery simulation** for the Improve phase, and an **SPC p-chart** for Control. Tooling: Python (pandas, numpy, scipy, matplotlib). Deterministic seed for full reproducibility.

## 7. Data sources

- **Real (cited):** the crisis timeline, root cause, demand-mix shift, and benchmarks — see `docs/references.md` (Business Standard, Outlook Business, Reuters, Open Magazine, Packaging South Asia; OTIF benchmarks from FourKites / MetricHQ; unit-economics anchor from Varun Beverages disclosures).
- **Simulated (transparent):** order-line distribution data generated by `src/generate_data.py`, calibrated to the above. Full data dictionary and assumptions in `docs/data_dictionary.md`.

## 8. Data collection process

The generator models the Bangalore network: 3 depots, ~520 customers across 4 channels (Quick-Commerce, Modern-Trade, General-Trade, HoReCa), 8 SKUs spanning CAN and PET formats. Each order line carries order/requested/actual dates, ordered vs delivered cases, on-time and in-full flags, failure reason, and per-case net realization and contribution margin. The aluminium-can shock is modelled as a time-localised availability factor from 15 Apr 2026 (sharp onset → trough → partial late-May recovery) that constrains CAN-format fulfilment only.

## 9. Data cleaning approach

Synthetic data is generated clean by construction; the pipeline still enforces type/parse checks (dates), derives `shortfall_cases`, `lost_revenue_inr`, `lost_margin_inr`, and validates that `delivered_cases ≤ ordered_cases`. On real client data, this phase would add duplicate removal, date-window validation, SKU-master joins, and outlier review.

## 10. Analysis

**10.1 Measure — the collapse.** Low-sugar OTIF: 88.8% → **20.8%**; fill rate → **60.0%**; process sigma 2.72 → **0.69** (≈792k DPMO). Pre-crisis OTIF (89%) was already **5.8 pp below** target. *(Fig 01)*

**10.2 Pareto — one cause dominates.** Of crisis OTIF failures, **packaging (can) unavailability = 86.8%** (12,974 of ~14,950 failed lines). Logistics reasons (late dispatch, route delay, vehicle) together are <10%. *(Fig 02)*

**10.3 Pack format is the fault line.** Crisis OTIF: **CAN = 3%**, **PET = 84.6%** (vs ~89% for both pre-crisis). Chi-square test of pack-format × OTIF in the crisis window: **χ² = 17,623, dof = 1, p < 0.001** — the association is overwhelming, not chance. *(Fig 03)*

**10.4 SKU view.** All four CAN SKUs collapse to ~49–54% fill; all four PET SKUs hold at ~99%. The problem is the *format*, not the brand. *(Fig 05)*

**10.5 Channel view.** Quick-Commerce (38.3%) and Modern-Trade (38.9%) suffer most — they are the most Diet-Coke / can-intensive and have the tightest delivery windows; General-Trade is least hit (47.1%). *(Fig 04)*

**10.6 Fishbone / 5-Whys (root cause).**
*Why did OTIF collapse?* Can SKUs could not be filled. *Why?* Depots had no cans. *Why?* Inbound can supply was rationed. *Why?* Aluminium can supply was shocked (Hormuz). *Why did that hit Diet Coke so hard?* **Because Diet Coke is ~95% single-format (can) with no PET/glass fallback and no substitution playbook** — a single point of failure. Root cause = **single-format + single-region sourcing concentration**, not the shock itself.

**10.7 FMEA (residual risk ranking).** Highest RPN items: single-format dependence (severity high, detection low), single-region aluminium sourcing, absence of demand-substitution logic in quick-commerce, and thin can buffer stock. These map directly to the interventions below.

## 11. Findings

1. The crisis was a **single-root-cause** event (86.8% one reason).
2. The damage was **format-determined** (CAN 3% vs PET 85%, p < 0.001).
3. The **controllable** loss (can shortfall that could be redirected/buffered) is **₹1.81 cr of the ₹1.83 cr** total — i.e. almost all of it is addressable by resilience design.
4. The **baseline service system was already below target**, a separate, ongoing opportunity.

## 12. Insights

- A high-growth portfolio (low-sugar) was run on a **fragile, undiversified packaging base** — growth outran resilience.
- Quick-commerce both **amplifies** the pain (tight windows, can-heavy) and **offers the cure** (real-time sell-out data + easy digital substitution).
- The cheapest, fastest lever (substitution playbook) is *operational*, not capital — ideal for Green-Belt execution.

## 13. Recommendations (RICE-prioritised)

| # | Intervention | Reach | Impact | Confidence | Effort | RICE | Recovered |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 2 | **Dynamic substitution playbook** (auto-offer Coke Zero PET when can OOS) | 9 | 8 | 0.8 | 2 | **28.8** | ₹0.45 cr |
| 1 | **Pack-format diversification** (Diet Coke 750 ml PET + 250 ml glass, Bangalore) | 8 | 9 | 0.7 | 5 | **10.1** | ₹0.63 cr |
| 3 | **Can buffer (4–6 wk) + 2nd supplier + early-warning index** | 7 | 8 | 0.7 | 6 | **6.5** | ₹0.36 cr |

Sequence: **start with #2** (fastest payback, lowest effort), run **#1** in parallel (line/format changeover), and stand up **#3** as the structural backstop. Combined recovery **≈₹1.45 cr (80%)**; crisis OTIF **20.8% → 81.1%**; structural target restored to **95%**.

## 14. Implementation roadmap

| Horizon | Actions | Owner | Success metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0–2 wks** | Launch substitution rules on quick-commerce + MT; define OTIF measurement SOP | Sales Ops / KAM | Substitution capture rate ≥ 25% |
| **2–8 wks** | Stand up supply-risk dashboard & early-warning thresholds; size can buffer | Supply Planning | Can days-of-cover ≥ 14 |
| **1–3 mo** | Fast-track Diet Coke PET/glass SKUs for Bangalore; qualify 2nd can supplier | Packaging / Procurement | PET share of Diet Coke ≥ 30% |
| **3–6 mo** | Embed control plan; roll out to other metros; review FMEA quarterly | Quality / Green Belt | OTIF ≥ 95%, in-control p-chart |

## 15. Risks & mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| PET cannibalises can margin / brand equity | Position PET as resilience SKU; phase by season; monitor mix |
| Substitution erodes Diet Coke loyalty | Offer *same-brand* zero (Coke Zero) first; track switch-back |
| 2nd supplier qualification lead time | Begin qualification now; interim buffer covers the gap |
| Another global commodity shock | Early-warning index + buffer cap recovery at ~80% by design |

## 16. Limitations

Transactional data is **simulated**, calibrated to public benchmarks — method conclusions are robust; absolute rupee figures are **illustrative**. The aluminium shock is physical; no analytics create cans, hence the deliberate 80% recovery ceiling. Recovery assumes partial pre-positioning; real PET changeover and supplier-qualification lead times apply.

## 17. Expected business impact

- **Service:** crisis OTIF 20.8% → 81.1%; structural path to 95% (in-control).
- **Margin:** ≈₹1.45 cr recovered per comparable six-week shock in Bangalore alone; materially larger if rolled across metros.
- **Resilience:** single-point-of-failure removed; quantified early-warning system installed.
- **Hygiene:** closes the 5.8 pp baseline gap via the measurement SOP and control plan.

## 18. Future scope

- Replace simulated data with real distributor OTIF feeds; re-baseline.
- Build a demand-sensing model on quick-commerce sell-out data.
- Extend FMEA to ingredient and PET-resin supply.
- Multi-metro rollout; tie buffer sizing to a live aluminium-price/lead-time index.
